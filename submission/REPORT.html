THE GIFT!

A tactile fashion-physics game for POBBOP

AMCC 5160 · Assignment 3 · November 2026

Concept and intended experience

Pitch

Drop reusable gift boxes to build a fragile tower across three increasingly windy stages.

Player feeling

Tactile tension with playful fashion-editorial feedback.

Core action

Read horizontal motion, choose the release moment, then watch real physics decide the result.

Three-minute arc

Learn on a wide platform, adapt to a visible crosswind, then survive two moving wind corridors.

Game structure

Title and one-line instruction
↓
10 points qualify the stage
↓
Spend all three lives
↓
Choose the next stage or end the run
↓
Results, local best score and share card

Route and workflow

Engine

Workflow

Brief → grey-box → physics debugging → visual system → sound and UI → browser tests → production build.

AI role

Codex assisted with implementation, diagnosis, tests and documentation. Image generation produced one original background asset.

Key prompts

  1. Build a complete POBBOP stacking game with real physics.
  2. Make every Space press release the current box immediately.
  3. Add three stages with smaller platforms and visible wind.
  4. Make tailwind accelerate and headwind slow each box.
  5. Align the deliverables with AMCC 5160 Assignment 3.

Two improvements

Release reliability

Before: some boxes froze after Space because sleeping bodies and static transitions disagreed.

After: every box remains dynamic, follows a kinematic rail, wakes explicitly and falls on the shared physics clock.

Readable challenge

Before: wind existed as an invisible number.

After: animated wind corridors show where force acts. Tailwind accelerates; headwind slows. Level and total scores are separate.

Reflection

I directed the project around one repeatable action: deciding when to release a moving box. The hardest part was not adding content but making the physical response predictable. Matter.js initially froze boxes because static bodies, sleeping and the render loop did not share a reliable release state. AI helped locate those implementation failures quickly and suggested testable alternatives. It was less useful when a fix appeared plausible without reproducing the full sequence of later boxes. I learned to ask for observable state, test the second and third attempt, and separate visual animation from the physics rules. I also chose to keep the POBBOP visual language restrained. The game uses cream, black, silver and lemon rather than familiar arcade colours. Wind became more successful after I turned it into a visible place in the world instead of a hidden difficulty multiplier. The current build has a clear start, escalating middle and player-chosen ending. I still need two human players to complete the required playtest evidence. Their feedback should decide the final wind strength and whether ten points per stage feels achievable within the intended three-to-ten-minute session.
